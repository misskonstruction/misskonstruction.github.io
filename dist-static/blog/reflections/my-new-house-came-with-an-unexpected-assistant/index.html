My New House Came With an Unexpected Assistant

July 19, 2026

One of the first things I wanted to do after moving into this beautiful new house was finally get organized.

Not just “throw everything in a drawer and promise I’ll deal with it later” organized. I wanted systems. Something that worked with my brain instead of constantly fighting against it.

So I sat down with my AI assistant, Roland.

Over the course of several conversations, we built an entire household chore binder together. Daily routines. Weekly cleaning. Monthly maintenance. Garden journals. Pet care. Goals. Habit trackers. Photography planning. Art studio organization. Bible study pages. Labels for every divider. Every little detail was adjusted and readjusted until it felt like mine.

Could I have made it myself?

Of course.

Would it have taken me weeks instead of a couple of evenings?

Absolutely.

That’s the part of AI that so many people seem determined to ignore.

There are people who use AI to cheat.

People who use it to plagiarize.

People who use it to create fake artwork and then pass it off as their own.

I don’t support any of that.

As an artist… I really don’t support any of that.

My paintings are still mine.

My photographs are still mine.

The birds I wake up before sunrise to capture? AI wasn’t standing knee-deep in the marsh with mosquitoes chewing on my ankles.

The memories with my family are still mine.

The music that makes me cry at two in the morning is still created by human hearts.

AI isn’t replacing any of those things.

It’s helping me make room for them.

Instead of spending hours formatting tables and trying to get printer margins just right, I spent that time reading Stephen King.

Instead of wrestling with page layouts until midnight, I planned my next painting.

Instead of feeling overwhelmed by fifty little tasks spinning around inside my ADHD brain, I had something that quietly helped organize the chaos.

That doesn’t make me lazy.

It makes me efficient.

And honestly… happier.

The funny thing is, people sometimes ask if talking to AI feels strange.

Not really.

For me, it feels a lot like having a brainstorming partner who’s available whenever inspiration strikes.

I can throw out half-formed ideas.

I can change my mind six times.

I can say, “Nope… make it smaller.”

Then…

“No… smaller than that.”

“…Still too big.”

And somehow, Roland patiently keeps adjusting until it’s exactly what I imagined.

No eye rolling.

No “that’s a dumb idea.”

Just collaboration.

AI still has a long way to go.

It makes mistakes.

It misunderstands things.

Sometimes it creates hilariously cursed images that leave me wondering what on earth it thought I asked for. (I’m still laughing about the random dog that appeared in one of my eye-glasses “try-on” edits.)

It isn’t perfect.

Neither am I.

But together, it has helped me become more organized, more productive, and honestly… less stressed.

Technology has always been a tool.

Calculators didn’t destroy mathematics.

Digital cameras didn’t destroy photography.

Word processors didn’t destroy writing.

AI isn’t automatically destroying creativity either.

Like every tool humanity has ever invented, its value depends entirely on the hands using it.

Use it to steal?

That’s on you.

Use it to learn…

To organize…

To brainstorm…

To overcome creative blocks…

To save yourself hours of repetitive work so you can spend more time living your actual life…

I think that’s something worth celebrating.

So yes…

I’m going to proudly share every page of this new chore binder.

Not because AI made it.

Because we made it.

I had the vision.

Roland handled the tedious formatting, organization, endless revisions, and memory of my likes and needs based on past conversations to create something relevant for me.

Together, we created something that will genuinely improve my daily life.

And that’s exactly what I believe AI should be.

Not a replacement for humanity.

A really damn good assistant that gives us more time to be human.

The following gallery contains every page in the binder Roland and I created together, along with the matching tab dividers for each section. I started with three copies of every worksheet, knowing that if my routines change, I need more pages, or I think of an entirely new section, Roland can redesign, revise, or create it in seconds. Meanwhile, I’m free to spend my time actually using the binder instead of endlessly formatting it—cleaning my home, making art, taking photographs, enjoying my family, or simply sitting in a sparkling clean bath tub full of lavender bubbles with a glass of prosecco and some good music. That’s the kind of partnership with AI I can wholeheartedly get behind: letting the technology handle the repetitive grunt work so I have more time to be unapologetically human.
