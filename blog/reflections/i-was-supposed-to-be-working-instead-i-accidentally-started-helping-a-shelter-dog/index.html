I Was Supposed to Be Working. Instead, I Accidentally Started Helping a Shelter Dog.

August 14, 2026

I would like to begin by saying that I absolutely had things I was supposed to be doing when I discovered this.

There are approximately 16 calls with random violations in the universe demanding my professional attention.

Instead, I have a dog named Pancake.

Technically, Pancake isn’t my dog. Pancake is a real shelter dog represented inside a little Android game called TREAT, where I roll dice, move an adorable virtual version of him around a board, collect treats and supplies, maintain his health, and occasionally yell at my phone because I need the damn dice to land on the dog treat.

(You know. Important IT/TL/PRT/QA stuff. Because my various job titles are designed to confuse even me.)

But here’s the thing:

The treats are real.

And so is Pancake.

Meet My New Dog, Pancake 🥹

I downloaded TREAT and was randomly paired with this beautiful little bully currently waiting for his forever family at a rescue near me.

That alone felt a little suspiciously targeted by the universe.

Then I started playing.

The concept is incredibly simple. You roll dice, move around a board and land on different spaces. You earn goodies for your assigned shelter dog and work toward filling a care package.

I had already managed to send Pancake free treats just by screwing around with the game when I stopped and thought:

Wait.

Are they actually sending this stuff?

Because I’ve been on the internet since dinosaurs roamed AOL chat rooms. I know better than to see something wholesome online and immediately assume everyone involved is actually wholesome.

So I did what any responsible adult would do.

I asked ChatGPT to investigate the company while I continued rolling dice.

This division of labor seemed appropriate.

So I Sent Roland Down the Rabbit Hole

Full disclosure before I go any further: I did not personally conduct the research in this section.

I was busy taking care of Pancake.

I asked my AI assistant Roland to investigate TREAT, its developers, business model, app-store presence and—most importantly—whether actual animal shelters could independently confirm receiving the things players supposedly earn.

And what came back surprised the hell out of me.

TREAT appears to be the real deal.

TREAT is operated by Treat App Ltd., and independent startup database Startup Nation Central identifies the company as a mobile gaming/pet-care startup founded in 2020 by Itay Katzav and Max Lerner. It reports that the company has raised approximately $2.42 million and describes its business model as “Play to Impact”: connecting mobile gaming with tangible support for real shelter animals.

The app isn’t tiny, either.

As of this writing, the Android version has surpassed one million downloads on Google Play, carries approximately a 4.8-star rating, and was updated as recently as July 8, 2026.

On Apple’s App Store, TREAT has roughly 60,000 ratings and a 4.9-star average.

Okay.

But downloads and stars weren’t what I cared about.

I wanted to know if the dogs were real and whether the shelters were actually getting anything.

The Dogs Are Real

This may be my favorite part.

According to TREAT, the shelter dogs appearing in the game are based on actual adoptable dogs, with TREAT creating their virtual versions using information from Petfinder.

So that picture of Pancake at the top of my game isn’t some stock-photo puppy designed to manipulate me into caring about a collection of pixels.

That’s Pancake.

There’s a real dog behind my cartoon dog.

And somewhere nearby, that real dog is waiting for somebody to realize he belongs in their family.

Well.

Fuck.

There goes any chance of me remaining emotionally detached from this game.

But Do The Shelters Actually Get Anything?

This was the question that mattered most to me.

TREAT says that players earn real treats, meals, medical items and other supplies through gameplay. Once care boxes are filled, the earned items are calculated and shipments are sent to participating shelters through Chewy.

That’s what the company says.

More importantly, I found independent confirmation from an actual participating rescue.

Almost Home Animal Rescue has its own page explaining TREAT to its supporters, and when answering the question of whether it really receives TREAT deliveries, the shelter says yes—it receives weekly deliveries of food, treats and medical supplies.

Almost Home actually promotes the game as another way people can support its animals.

That was the piece of information I needed.

Because a company telling me “Look how much good we’re doing!” is advertising.

A shelter saying “Yep, boxes keep showing up” is something else entirely.

One Important Clarification

When I earn a treat for Pancake, that doesn’t necessarily mean someone walks into his kennel holding the exact Milk-Bone Cami earned Tuesday when she should have been answering Jira tickets.

Which is probably for the best.

Participating shelters explain that TREAT combines the food and treats players earn into larger weekly shipments. The supplies benefit the shelter caring for your dog rather than necessarily being individually earmarked for that particular animal.

And honestly?

That makes more sense.

If Pancake has enough food today but the terrified little shepherd three kennels down needs it, feed the shepherd.

My emotional attachment may be to Pancake, but my goal is helping the rescue keep animals fed and cared for while they wait for homes.

And Then One Day, Pancake Is Supposed to Leave Me

This might be the most beautifully cruel game mechanic anyone has ever invented.

TREAT tracks the real-world adoption status of its shelter dogs.

When your dog finds a home, you get notified.

Your little virtual shelter dog disappears from this strange relationship you’ve built because the actual dog doesn’t need you anymore.

He has a family.

TREAT describes that as adding another dog to your “Rescued” count.

So instead of beating a boss or unlocking Level 700 or receiving 47 imaginary gemstones…

you find out Pancake went home.

I’m going to be a fucking wreck.

I already know this.

And I will happily be a fucking wreck.

This Is a Business, Not a Charity

I think this distinction is important if I’m going to recommend something to people.

TREAT is a for-profit company, not something I’m presenting to you as a nonprofit animal charity.

It has a freemium business model. You can play for free, while optional purchases and subscriptions provide things like additional daily rolls, unlimited deliveries and meal plans for individual dogs. TREAT calls the latter “remote fostering.”

Its App Store listing likewise clearly identifies in-app purchases.

And I’m completely okay with that.

Somebody figured out how to take a thing millions of us already do—waste questionable amounts of time tapping colorful shit on our phones—and connect some of that activity to food, treats and supplies for homeless animals.

Please monetize my inability to put my phone down if part of the result is feeding a shelter dog.

I have spent time on far stupider things.

The Numbers Are Kind of Nuts

TREAT currently says players have earned and sent more than 40 million meals, treats and medical items to shelter animals through its platform and that more than 20,000 dogs associated with the program have subsequently been rescued/adopted.

Those are TREAT’s own reported figures, not numbers I’m claiming were independently audited, and I think that distinction matters.

But there’s something interesting in the historical data, too.

Startup Nation Central reported in January 2025 that the company’s model had already facilitated more than 170,000 items for shelter animals.

Whatever else you think of the concept, this little game has clearly grown.

Is Everything Perfect?

No.

Because nothing involving software ever is.

And as someone who works in IT, I would be immediately suspicious if it were.

There are user complaints involving subscriptions, support responsiveness and dogs occasionally showing incorrect adoption statuses. Some recent Google Play reviewers have reported exactly those sorts of problems.

Interestingly, an actual participating shelter acknowledges the adoption-status problem too: because TREAT pulls information from Petfinder, there can sometimes be a delay or glitch between a dog’s real-world adoption and the status displayed in the game.

So this isn’t me telling you the app is perfect.

I’m telling you I went looking for evidence that the entire feel-good premise was bullshit, and instead found an actual shelter confirming that the supplies arrive.

That’s good enough for me to tell you about it.

So…This Is Pancake ❤️

For now, this little guy is mine.

Not really mine.

But mine enough.

I’ll roll my dice. I’ll keep his health up. I’ll fill his boxes. I’ll send whatever free goodies the game lets me earn. And I’ll probably become embarrassingly attached to a dog I’ve never met.

Then hopefully one day I’ll open this stupid adorable game and discover the absolute best possible bad news:

I lost Pancake.

Because somebody saw that face and took him home.

Until then, apparently I have another dog to take care of.

And somewhere, buried beneath Pancake and my dice rolls, there are still approximately 16 calls waiting for me to evaluate.

They’ll be fine.

Pancake needed a treat.

Want to Help a Shelter Dog Too?

You can learn more directly from TREAT’s official website, or find the app on Google Play for Android and the Apple App Store.

I played the game and fell in love with Pancake. The robot did the homework.
