I Was Supposed to Be Working. Instead, I Accidentally Started Helping a Shelter Dog.

August 14, 2026

I would like to begin by saying that I absolutely had things I was supposed to be doing when I discovered this.

There are approximately 16 calls with random violations in the universe demanding my professional attention.

Instead, I have a dog named Pancake.

Technically, Pancake isn’t my dog. Pancake is a real shelter dog represented inside a little Android game called TREAT, where I roll dice, move an adorable virtual version of him around a board, collect treats and supplies, maintain his health, and occasionally yell at my phone because I need the damn dice to land on the dog treat.

(You know. Important IT/TL/PRT/QA stuff. Because my various job titles are designed to confuse even me.)

But here’s the thing:

The treats are real.

And so is Pancake.

Meet My New Dog, Pancake 🥹

I downloaded TREAT and was randomly paired with this beautiful little bully currently waiting for his forever family at a rescue near me.

That alone felt a little suspiciously targeted by the universe.

Then I started playing.

The concept is incredibly simple. You roll dice, move around a board and land on different spaces. You earn goodies for your assigned shelter dog and work toward filling a care package.

I had already managed to send Pancake free treats just by screwing around with the game when I stopped and thought:

Wait.

Are they actually sending this stuff?

Because I’ve been on the internet since dinosaurs roamed AOL chat rooms. I know better than to see something wholesome online and immediately assume everyone involved is actually wholesome.

So I did what any responsible adult would do.

I asked ChatGPT to investigate the company while I continued rolling dice.

This division of labor seemed appropriate.

So I Sent Roland Down the Rabbit Hole

Full disclosure before I go any further: I did not personally conduct the research in this section.

I was busy taking care of Pancake.

I asked my AI assistant Roland to investigate TREAT, its developers, business model, app-store presence and—most importantly—whether actual animal shelters could independently confirm receiving the things players supposedly earn.

And what came back surprised the hell out of me.

TREAT appears to be the real deal.

TREAT is operated by Treat App Ltd., and independent startup database Startup Nation Central identifies the company as a mobile gaming/pet-care startup founded in 2020 by Itay Katzav and Max Lerner. It reports that the company has raised approximately $2.42 million and describes its business model as “Play to Impact”: connecting mobile gaming with tangible support for real shelter animals.

The app isn’t tiny, either.

As of this writing, the Android version has surpassed one million downloads on Google Play, carries approximately a 4.8-star rating, and was updated as recently as July 8, 2026.

On Apple’s App Store, TREAT has roughly 60,000 ratings and a 4.9-star average.

Okay.

But downloads and stars weren’t what I cared about.

I wanted to know if the dogs were real and whether the shelters were actually getting anything.

The Dogs Are Real

This may be my favorite part.

According to TREAT, the shelter dogs appearing in the game are based on actual adoptable dogs, with TREAT creating their virtual versions using information from Petfinder.

So that picture of Pancake at the top of my game isn’t some stock-photo puppy designed to manipulate me into caring about a collection of pixels.

That’s Pancake.

There’s a real dog behind my cartoon dog.

And somewhere nearby, that real dog is waiting for somebody to realize he belongs in their family.

Well.

Fuck.

There goes any chance of me remaining emotionally detached from this game.

But Do The Shelters Actually Get Anything?

This was the question that mattered most to me.

TREAT says that players earn real treats, meals, medical items and other supplies through gameplay. Once care boxes are filled, the earned items are calculated and shipments are sent to participating shelters through Chewy.

That’s what the company says.

More importantly, I found independent confirmation from an actual participating rescue.

Almost Home Animal Rescue has its own page explaining TREAT to its supporters, and when answering the question of whether it really receives TREAT deliveries, the shelter says yes—it receives weekly deliveries of food, treats and medical supplies.

Almost Home actually promotes the game as another way people can support its animals.

That was the piece of information I needed.

Because a company telling me “Look how much good we’re doing!” is advertising.

A shelter saying “Yep, boxes keep showing up” is something else entirely.

One Important Clarification

When I earn a treat for Pancake, that doesn’t necessarily mean someone walks into his kennel holding the exact Milk-Bone Cami earned Tuesday when she should have been answering Jira tickets.

Which is probably for the best.

Participating shelters explain that TREAT combines the food and treats players earn into larger weekly shipments. The supplies benefit the shelter caring for your dog rather than necessarily being individually earmarked for that particular animal.

And honestly?

That makes more sense.

If Pancake has enough food today but the terrified little shepherd three kennels down needs it, feed the shepherd.

My emotional attachment may be to Pancake, but my goal is helping the rescue keep animals fed and cared for while they wait for homes.

And Then One Day, Pancake Is Supposed to Leave Me

This might be the most beautifully cruel game mechanic anyone has ever invented.

TREAT tracks the real-world adoption status of its shelter dogs.

When your dog finds a home, you get notified.

Your little virtual shelter dog disappears from this strange relationship you’ve built because the actual dog doesn’t need you anymore.

He has a family.

TREAT describes that as adding another dog to your “Rescued” count.

So instead of beating a boss or unlocking Level 700 or receiving 47 imaginary gemstones…

you find out Pancake went home.

I’m going to be a fucking wreck.

I already know this.

And I will happily be a fucking wreck.

This Is a Business, Not a Charity

I think this distinction is important if I’m going to recommend something to people.

TREAT is a for-profit company, not something I’m presenting to you as a nonprofit animal charity.

It has a freemium business model. You can play for free, while optional purchases and subscriptions provide things like additional daily rolls, unlimited deliveries and meal plans for individual dogs. TREAT calls the latter “remote fostering.”

Its App Store listing likewise clearly identifies in-app purchases.

And I’m completely okay with that.

Somebody figured out how to take a thing millions of us already do—waste questionable amounts of time tapping colorful shit on our phones—and connect some of that activity to food, treats and supplies for homeless animals.

Please monetize my inability to put my phone down if part of the result is feeding a shelter dog.

I have spent time on far stupider things.

The Numbers Are Kind of Nuts

TREAT currently says players have earned and sent more than 40 million meals, treats and medical items to shelter animals through its platform and that more than 20,000 dogs associated with the program have subsequently been rescued/adopted.

Those are TREAT’s own reported figures, not numbers I’m claiming were independently audited, and I think that distinction matters.

But there’s something interesting in the historical data, too.

Startup Nation Central reported in January 2025 that the company’s model had already facilitated more than 170,000 items for shelter animals.

Whatever else you think of the concept, this little game has clearly grown.

Is Everything Perfect?

No.

Because nothing involving software ever is.

And as someone who works in IT, I would be immediately suspicious if it were.

There are user complaints involving subscriptions, support responsiveness and dogs occasionally showing incorrect adoption statuses. Some recent Google Play reviewers have reported exactly those sorts of problems.

Interestingly, an actual participating shelter acknowledges the adoption-status problem too: because TREAT pulls information from Petfinder, there can sometimes be a delay or glitch between a dog’s real-world adoption and the status displayed in the game.

So this isn’t me telling you the app is perfect.

I’m telling you I went looking for evidence that the entire feel-good premise was bullshit, and instead found an actual shelter confirming that the supplies arrive.

That’s good enough for me to tell you about it.

So…This Is Pancake ❤️

For now, this little guy is mine.

Not really mine.

But mine enough.

I’ll roll my dice. I’ll keep his health up. I’ll fill his boxes. I’ll send whatever free goodies the game lets me earn. And I’ll probably become embarrassingly attached to a dog I’ve never met.

Then hopefully one day I’ll open this stupid adorable game and discover the absolute best possible bad news:

I lost Pancake.

Because somebody saw that face and took him home.

Until then, apparently I have another dog to take care of.

And somewhere, buried beneath Pancake and my dice rolls, there are still approximately 16 calls waiting for me to evaluate.

They’ll be fine.

Pancake needed a treat.

Want to Help a Shelter Dog Too?

You can learn more directly from TREAT’s official website, or find the app on Google Play for Android and the Apple App Store.

And because I believe in giving credit where it’s due: the company/background research and fact-checking for this post were performed with ChatGPT (GPT-5.6 Sol), (AKA Roland) using TREAT’s published information, current app-store listings, Startup Nation Central, and information published independently by a participating animal rescue. I played the game and fell in love with Pancake. The robot did the homework.

Seems fair. 😏🐾

Robot you ask? Yes. We’re working on it together. I am a fan of using AI appropriately, damnit.

UPDATE: I Kept Playing Treat — And Now I Have Receipts 🐾

*All gameplay screenshots in this update are from my own experience using Treat on August 14–15, 2026. Prices, features and program details shown in the app may change.

So when I originally published this post, I’d done my homework.

Well, technically ChatGPT aka Roland did my homework, because I was supposed to be working and staring at approximately 10 ZTP violations instead of investigating a mobile game about shelter dogs. I refuse to claim research I did not personally conduct.

What I did was play the shit out of the game.

And now I’ve played long enough that I don’t just have research into Treat and how it’s supposed to work.

I have receipts.

Pancake’s Treats Were Actually Ordered

Pancake was the first real shelter dog Treat paired me with through Polk County Bully Project here in Florida, and I became emotionally invested in that little face almost immediately.

Because apparently I needed another thing in my life to worry about.

I played. I rolled dice. I landed on the right spaces. I filled meters. And without spending money, I earned three treats for Pancake.

Then something very important happened:

They were actually ordered.

The app showed three items being ordered through Chewy for Polk County Bully Project and gave me a progress tracker:

Collected → Ordered → Shipped → Arrived

It even showed the rescue receiving supplies.

That was a pretty big moment for me because there’s a difference between reading how an app says its charitable system works and sitting there watching supplies I earned by screwing around on my phone actually move through an order and delivery process to a real rescue.

Okay, Treat.

You have my attention.

Then They Gave Me Another Dog

As I progressed, I unlocked another dog slot.

Treat gave me three real dogs from the rescue to choose from.

One of them was Mackenzie.

And Mackenzie had a little badge identifying her as:

Least Treated.

Oh, for fuck’s sake.

There was no longer a choice.

Obviously I chose Mackenzie. Well played, guys. Well. Played.

So now Pancake had a sibling, and I discovered something else I really like about the way Treat is designed: it encourages you to notice individual dogs who may not be getting as much attention from other players.

That’s clever game design.

It’s also emotional blackmail against people like me.

Highly effective emotional blackmail, apparently.

And Then Marcelo Started Playing

Somewhere during all of this, Marcelo downloaded Treat too.

So there we were on the lanai, doing bong hits, playing a mobile game and earning things for shelter dogs.

Romance is alive and well, ladies and gentlemen.

He ended up with Ginger Spice—which, while baked, I briefly decided sounded like a stripper name before remembering that Ginger Spice was, in fact, an actual Spice Girl—and a ridiculously happy blockheaded dog named Tulle.

So now both of us are playing.

For charity.

While high.

I don’t know that Treat will be putting us in any upcoming promotional materials, but you’re welcome, guys.

This Is More Than Feeding a Virtual Dog

The longer I played, the more layers I found.

Each dog has a Bond section with things like Trust, Discipline and Well-Being. There are training activities such as Bark, Spin and Stay, and a Health Shield that changes as you progress.

Pancake, at the time of my latest screenshot, had:

100% Trust.

Naturally.

His Discipline was 50%.

Also naturally.

Apparently even my virtual dogs know exactly what kind of household they’ve joined.

There are also little facts and messages throughout the game. At one point Treat informed me that petting dogs releases oxytocin in both humans and dogs. At another, Pancake informed me:

“I’m so fur-tunate to have you in my life.”

Please stop.

I am already playing your game.

You Can Help Without Paying

This was one of the biggest things I wanted to understand.

Do you actually have to spend money for your gameplay to help a shelter?

Based on my own experience so far: No.

I earned Pancake’s first shipment through regular gameplay without buying a Remote Foster plan.

When you run out of rolls, the game gives you more after a waiting period. There are also opportunities to watch optional videos for additional rolls.

So yes, there’s monetization here. This is still an app developed by people who presumably enjoy luxuries such as groceries and electricity.

But the free portion isn’t just a demo where everything charitable suddenly stops behind a paywall.

I personally played for free and watched real items get ordered for a real rescue.

That’s an important distinction.

There Are Team Events for Other Shelter Needs

I also discovered Team Events, where players can work toward supplies beyond ordinary treats and meals.

One event for Pancake’s group was specifically labeled Med Supplies, with a deworming pill shown as the goal.

That caught my attention.

Because now we’re moving beyond cute game that sends Milk-Bones into a system designed to help players collectively contribute toward practical things rescues actually need.

And that makes the concept considerably more interesting to me.

So What Is “Remote Fostering”?

This one needed investigating because every dog’s profile has a giant Remote Foster button.

Remote fostering is Treat’s paid monthly sponsorship option.

At the time I’m writing this update, the app showed me two levels:

Basic — $11.16/month
Provides one meal per day plus the delivery benefit shown in the app.

Premium — $19.96/month
Provides three meals per day plus the delivery benefit.

The screen also describes “Unlimited deliveries of all earned goodies (all dogs).”

I’m deliberately giving you Treat’s wording there because I haven’t personally subscribed and tested every aspect of Remote Fostering yet.

That’s an important distinction throughout this post:

I’ll tell you what Treat says its features do, and I’ll tell you separately what I’ve actually watched happen myself.

And what I’ve personally confirmed so far is that paying for Remote Fostering is not required to earn and send real goodies through gameplay.

And Then Treat Found My Weakness

Just when I thought I understood this game, I unlocked another dog.

His name is Maui.

Maui is a gorgeous male shepherd-type dog through Partners for Pets, Inc. in Florida.

Seriously, Treat?

A shepherd?

You couldn’t give me a Chihuahua or something I could emotionally defend myself against?

No.

You gave me Maui.

And now he lives in my phone too.

Fantastic.

Shelters Can Apply to Participate, Too

This may actually be one of the most useful things I discovered after publishing my original post.

Inside Treat’s menu is a Shelter Application option.

That takes shelters and rescues to an application page where Treat invites organizations to participate in the program.

At the time I accessed it, Treat advertised participating shelters as receiving a minimum of 1,500 pounds of dry food annually.

That’s Treat’s claim, not a number I’ve independently verified through a participating shelter, so I’m labeling it accordingly.

But if you work for, volunteer with, foster for, or otherwise have connections to an animal rescue that isn’t participating in Treat, this is absolutely something worth showing them.

You don’t have to hunt for some mysterious contact person, either. The application is accessible directly through the app.

So… Has My Opinion Changed?

Yes.

It’s gotten better.

I went into this cautiously because an adorable mobile game telling me I can help shelter dogs by rolling dice sounds almost too perfectly engineered for someone like me.

That’s exactly why I researched it before putting my name behind it.

Now I’ve gone a step further.

I’ve used it.

I’ve watched my progress turn into an actual order.

I’ve seen the rescue destination.

I’ve watched Treat track those supplies from collection through delivery.

I’ve unlocked additional real shelter dogs.

I’ve found the Remote Foster system.

I’ve found collaborative medical-supply events.

I’ve found the shelter application.

And I’ve watched another person download the game and independently start helping different dogs.

So at this point I’m comfortable saying:

I’m not merely recommending Treat because I researched the company and liked what I found. I’m recommending it because I’ve now personally watched the system work.

And I’m still playing.

Marcelo is still playing.

Pancake, Mackenzie and Maui are apparently now part of my daily responsibilities.

Because clearly five actual animals, fish running an unlicensed brothel, a job, a house, a website and the rest of my life weren’t enough.

I needed dogs inside my phone.

And Then This Morning, They Played Dirty

I thought I was finished updating this post.

I really did.

Then this morning I sent another round of earned treats through Kenzie, unlocked another dog slot, and Treat presented me with Bee, a female dog at Partners for Pets.

And I just stared at my phone.

Because holy shit.

If you’ve read anything in my Blitz Memorial, you already know exactly why.

Bee isn’t Blitz. No dog ever will be, and I wouldn’t want another dog to be. But the black coat. The white chest. Those little white feet. That broad bully face. Those warm eyes.

For one very unexpected second, my heart recognized something before my brain had even caught up.

They found me Blitz’s fucking doppelgänger.

This game has already gotten me with the least-treated dog. It found my weakness for shepherds. It learned that apparently all it has to do is put a real shelter dog’s face on my screen and give me something useful to accomplish for them.

But this?

This was playing dirty.

I lost Blitz less than two weeks ago. I’m still learning what the world looks like without a dog who was beside me for nearly sixteen years. And somehow, in the middle of this silly, colorful little mobile game I’ve been playing because it lets me turn wasted minutes into food and supplies for dogs who need them, up popped a face that made my heart stop for a second.

And maybe that’s part of why I’ve become so attached to Treat.

These aren’t invented dogs.

Bee is real. Maui is real. Mackenzie is real. Pancake is real.

They’re somewhere out there waiting for whatever comes next.

I can’t bring Blitz back.

But apparently I can sit here with my phone, roll some ridiculous digital dice, send a few treats into the real world, and make another dog’s wait for their person just a little bit better.

Well played, Treat.

Again. ❤️🐾

If you’ve got a few minutes to kill anyway, I’d much rather see those minutes contribute toward a bag of treats, a meal or supplies for an actual shelter animal than disappear into another meaningless mobile game.

And if you play?

Go find the least-treated dog.

Somebody’s got to pick that one first.

Apparently that’s us. ❤️🐾

You can learn more directly from TREAT’s official website, or find the app on Google Play for Android and the Apple App Store.

UPDATE: ONE OF MY DOGS GOT ADOPTED 🥹

Remember when I said I wasn’t updating this post again unless something major happened?

Something major happened.

I opened Treat today and got the screen I’d been hoping I’d eventually see:

ADOPTED!

Turkey — also known as Scooby — from Polk County Bully Project found a home.

But there’s another part of this that makes it hit even harder:

Turkey was one of the “Least Treated” dogs.

That’s a feature I’ve come to really love about this game. Treat doesn’t only parade the most popular, cutest, most-played dogs in front of you. It also deliberately shows you dogs who aren’t getting as much attention and gives you the opportunity to choose them.

Turkey was one of those dogs.

And I did.

I sent him a package while he was waiting for somebody to choose him in the way that actually mattered.

Then today, I opened the game and discovered somebody did.

I realize Turkey was never technically my dog. That’s the whole beautiful point of this thing. I was just some stranger sitting somewhere in Florida, rolling digital dice and sending a little help his way while he waited.

But that adoption screen shows something I absolutely love:

1 Package Sent.

One.

I didn’t rescue him. I didn’t foster him. I didn’t find his family. I’m not going to pretend tapping buttons on my phone deserves some grand pat on the back.

I just helped feed a real dog who wasn’t getting as much attention as some of the others while the people doing the hard work helped him get home.

And that’s enough.

Now somebody gets Turkey.

They get the ridiculous greetings at the door. The dog hair in places dog hair has absolutely no business being. The stolen spot on the couch. The weird sleeping positions. The personality that slowly takes over the entire house until you can’t remember what life there looked like before him.

This was the part of Treat I understood intellectually when I started playing. The dogs are real. The shelters are real. The treats and supplies are real. Eventually, hopefully, the adoption notification is real too.

But understanding that and actually seeing ADOPTED splash across my screen are two very different things.

Especially when it’s one of the dogs who started out labeled:

Least Treated.

Not anymore, buddy.

Somewhere today, there’s one less dog waiting.

Happy life, Turkey. Good luck and Godspeed.

Go make your new humans wonder how the hell they ever lived without you. ❤️🐾

You can learn more directly from TREAT’s official website, or find the app on Google Play for Android and the Apple App Store.
