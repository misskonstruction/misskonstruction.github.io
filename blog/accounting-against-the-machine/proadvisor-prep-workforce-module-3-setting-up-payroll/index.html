ProAdvisor Prep: Workforce — Module 3: Setting Up Payroll

October 20, 2026

Welcome back, Resistance Accountants.

In Module 2, we worked through payroll taxes, employee classifications, overtime, and the difference between what employees earn and what employers actually owe.

Now we’re going to set up the system responsible for calculating and tracking all of that.

Which means we’re entering the part of QuickBooks where one incorrect tax rate, one overlooked employee work location, or one poorly planned payday can create a problem that won’t politely disappear when you close the browser.

Gladys has volunteered to supervise.

Wallace has brought the setup checklist.

Gertrude has informed us that “I thought QuickBooks handled that automatically” is not an acceptable explanation during an audit.

She’s a delight.

Before You Start Clicking Things

Payroll setup is not a race to the Run Payroll button.

Before starting, gather the information you’ll need to establish the employer, its employees, its tax obligations, and its payment arrangements.

That generally includes:

  • Business legal name, address, and federal Employer Identification Number (EIN).
  • Principal officer information required for payroll banking.
  • Applicable state and local tax registrations, account numbers, deposit schedules, and assigned rates.
  • Employee names, addresses, work locations, withholding information, and pay details.
  • Pay schedules and direct deposit information.
  • Year-to-date payroll history if employees have already been paid during the current year.

That last item deserves attention.

A business starting QuickBooks Payroll in October may not be starting payroll for the first time.

If employees have already received wages this year, their earlier earnings, deductions, and taxes may need to be entered correctly so year-to-date calculations and reporting are accurate.

Don’t treat a new QuickBooks subscription as a new tax year.

Step 1: Set Up the Employer

Start with the company’s identifying information.

QuickBooks needs to know which business is paying employees and which tax obligations apply.

The setup process may ask for the employer’s legal details, federal tax identification, business address, and authorized principal officer.

These aren’t decorative fields.

They’re used in payroll processing, tax reporting, and banking verification.

Make sure the information agrees with the employer’s actual registration documents.

And before adding employees, establish where the business operates and where its employees work.

Step 2: State Payroll Taxes — Location Matters

Let’s say your client operates a business in Florida.

They hire an employee who lives and works remotely in Georgia.

Congratulations. Your client now has a multistate payroll question.

But don’t assume QuickBooks can determine every legal obligation from the employee’s home address alone.

Depending on the circumstances, relevant factors include:

  • Where the employee physically performs the work.
  • Where the employee resides.
  • State income-tax withholding requirements.
  • State unemployment insurance rules.
  • Applicable reciprocity agreements.
  • State or local employer registration obligations.

The state withholding jurisdiction and state unemployment jurisdiction aren’t necessarily determined by the same rule.

The software needs accurate information, but the legal obligation must be established first.

Intuit specifically advises contacting the relevant state and local tax agencies when determining which taxes apply.

State Account Numbers and SUTA Rates

Employers may need separate state account numbers for different payroll tax programs.

They may also need an assigned state unemployment insurance rate.

And no, we’re not entering a rate because somebody vaguely remembers seeing one on a letter three years ago.

Use the applicable agency notice or confirmed rate.

A state unemployment rate may change, and an incorrect rate can affect the employer’s payroll tax calculations.

Here’s an important distinction:

QuickBooks can allow state tax setup before the employer has received every required account number.

However, the employer may need to pay taxes and file forms manually until the necessary information is entered and the supported electronic services are available.

That is very different from saying the entire payroll system automatically locks.

For the current Workforce workflow, Intuit documents state-tax settings under:

Settings → Payroll Settings → [State] tax section → Edit

The available sections depend on the taxes and jurisdictions involved.

Step 3: Connect the Payroll Bank Account

Now we need somewhere for the payroll money to come from.

Direct deposit requires an eligible business bank account that supports ACH transactions.

QuickBooks may offer an instant bank-verification option.

If that succeeds, the employer may be able to proceed without waiting for a separate test transaction.

If instant verification isn’t available or doesn’t succeed, a manual verification process may be required.

What About Those Tiny Bank Transactions?

Yes, Intuit uses small test transactions in some bank-verification workflows.

But don’t assume every customer receives two deposits.

For the current QuickBooks Workforce instructions, Intuit describes a test transaction of less than $1.00 and a process for entering the verified amount.

The documented route includes:

All apps → Payroll → Overview → Check your bank account → Verify Amount

An alternative route is:

Settings → Payroll Settings → Bank accounts → Edit → Verify Amount

The options shown depend on the account’s setup status and product interface.

Never invent a verification amount. Never enter an amount that hasn’t cleared.

And don’t assume that simply connecting a bank account means the first payroll is ready to submit.

The employer still needs to meet the applicable direct deposit processing deadline.

These are typical counts; calendar timing can produce occasional exceptions, such as a 53rd weekly or 27th biweekly payday.

Biweekly and semimonthly are not the same thing.

Biweekly means every two weeks.

Semimonthly means twice per month, often on specified dates.

That difference affects payday planning, cash flow, and how certain payroll amounts are calculated.

The pay schedule also needs to comply with applicable state payday requirements.

Step 5: Define the Workweek for Overtime

Here’s where we need to separate two concepts that people frequently combine.

The pay period tells us which period of earnings is being paid.

The workweek establishes the recurring seven-day period used for federal weekly overtime calculations.

Those are not necessarily the same boundaries.

Under federal law, an employer’s workweek is a fixed, regularly recurring period of 168 consecutive hours.

It can begin on any day and at any hour.

It doesn’t have to begin Monday morning.

And it doesn’t automatically reset on payday.

For QuickBooks Online, Intuit currently documents the week-start setting under:

Settings → Account and settings → Time → First day of work week

Workforce Premium and Elite also have a documented employee pay-type workflow for setting the workweek start day used in overtime calculations.

The setting can be changed, but that doesn’t mean employers should change the legally established workweek casually to avoid paying overtime.

Set the correct workweek. Configure overtime appropriately. Verify the results.

And remember that some states have additional overtime requirements beyond the federal weekly rule.

Step 6: Configure Payroll Accounting

Now for something our Level 2 accountants should recognize.

Payroll doesn’t just produce paychecks.

It creates accounting activity.

Wage expenses, employer payroll taxes, employee withholding liabilities, employer contributions, and payments all need to be recorded appropriately.

QuickBooks creates default payroll accounts, but supported accounting preferences can be customized.

For QuickBooks Online Payroll, Intuit documents:

Settings → Payroll Settings → Accounting → Edit

You may be able to map relevant payroll amounts to appropriate expense, liability, or other supported accounts.

But don’t assume every business should use the same chart of accounts.

For example, classifying wages as Cost of Goods Sold may be appropriate for certain direct labor costs, but it isn’t automatically appropriate for every employee’s compensation.

The goal isn’t to create as many payroll accounts as possible.

The goal is accurate, useful financial reporting.

And remember: changing payroll accounting preferences can affect how transactions appear in financial reports. Review the accounting treatment before making changes.

Three Payroll Setup Traps

Trap 1: Guessing the unemployment tax rate

The client says, “I think it’s around 2.7%.”

That’s not verification.

Find the applicable agency notice, confirm the correct period, and enter the assigned rate.

Trap 2: Assuming bank connection means payroll is ready

An instant connection, a pending test transaction, and a fully verified payroll bank account are not interchangeable statuses.

Confirm the actual verification status and direct deposit deadline before promising a payday.

Trap 3: Confusing pay schedules with overtime workweeks

Paying employees every two weeks does not mean overtime is calculated over an 80-hour period under ordinary federal weekly overtime rules.

An employee who works 48 hours one week and 32 the next generally cannot have those hours averaged together to eliminate the first week’s overtime obligation.

Scavenger Hunt Answers

Mission 1

Confirm whether the bank account was instantly verified or requires a test transaction.

For manual verification, check the cleared transaction amount against the bank statement.

Intuit documents the verification option under Payroll Overview’s Check your bank account task and an alternative under Payroll Settings → Bank accounts.

Successful instant verification does not require completing the separate test-transaction procedure.

Mission 2

Establish the employee’s residence, actual work location, applicable state and local payroll tax requirements, and employer registration obligations.

Consult the relevant state tax and unemployment agencies.

QuickBooks may allow state tax setup without all account numbers, but electronic filing and payment capabilities may be limited until the information is supplied.

Mission 3

For QuickBooks Online, go to Settings → Account and settings → Time and locate the first day of the workweek.

The pay schedule determines how often wages are paid; the workweek is the recurring period relevant to federal weekly overtime.

The workweek must reflect a legitimate, established schedule rather than a manipulation intended to avoid overtime.

Mission 4

Review Settings → Payroll Settings → Accounting, where available for the subscription and accounting configuration.

Confirm the appropriate accounts and reporting treatment with the business’s accountant before changing mappings.

One Last Check Before the First Payroll

Before the client presses the button, confirm that the employer and employee details are correct, applicable tax settings have been reviewed, the bank account is ready, the pay schedule is appropriate, and any prior payroll history has been entered.

Then review the actual payroll calculations.

Especially the first time.

Because setting up payroll isn’t just about getting QuickBooks to accept the information.

It’s about making sure the information deserves to be accepted.
