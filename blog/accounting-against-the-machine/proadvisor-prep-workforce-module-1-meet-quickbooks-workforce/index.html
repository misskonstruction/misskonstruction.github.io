ProAdvisor Prep: Workforce — Module 1: Meet QuickBooks Workforce

October 3, 2026

So we’re doing payroll now.

Yep.

Because finishing Level 1 and Level 2 apparently wasn’t enough. And I appreciate that.

Welcome to QuickBooks Workforce, Resistance Accountants!

And before we start clicking on shit, there’s an important distinction to make: Workforce isn’t just the little employee website some of us remember where somebody went to retrieve a pay stub or W-2.

Intuit has been consolidating payroll, employee information, time tracking and HR-related tools under the QuickBooks Workforce name. Employees can use Workforce through the web or mobile app to access things such as pay information, tax documents and—depending on the employer’s products and configuration—time tracking and other workforce features.

Consider this:

Human Capital Management.

That doesn’t mean every Workforce customer automatically gets every HR feature we’re about to discuss.

Plans and subscriptions matter.

A lot.

So we’re going to learn the capabilities without pretending everybody who opens Workforce has unlocked the entire fucking HR amusement park.

Employee Self-Service: Let the Human Enter Their Own Human Information

One of the genuinely useful ideas behind Workforce is employee self-service.

An employer can invite a worker to Workforce. The employee accepts the invitation using an existing Intuit account or creates one, and—where their employer’s payroll service supports it—can provide personal, tax and bank information themselves. Intuit specifically notes that the availability of those setup fields depends on the payroll service the employer uses.

That matters.

Instead of:

Employee → sensitive information → somebody else manually retypes it → QuickBooks

we can potentially have:

Employee → secure Workforce setup → QuickBooks

Fewer transcription opportunities.

Less passing sensitive information around unnecessarily.

And fewer chances for somebody to turn:

1234 Main Street

into:

1234 Maine Street

and create an entirely new citizen of fucking Vermont.

But Self-Service Does Not Mean “Employer Responsibility Has Left the Building”

This is particularly important with employment eligibility.

QuickBooks Workforce Premium and Elite currently support a digital Form I-9 workflow. The employee completes their portion, but the employer still has responsibilities—including reviewing the employee’s identification/employment-authorization documents and completing the employer section.

So:

Employee self-service = shared workflow.

It does not mean:

Send invitation → payroll robot handles federal employment law → go fishing.

Documents: More Than Pay Stubs

Workforce now has considerably more document capability than the old mental model of “place where employees get their W-2.”

Payroll admins can upload and manage employee records within employee profiles, creating centralized storage for contracts, hiring forms and other documentation.

And some Workforce subscriptions go further.

Document Templates

Current Workforce Premium and Elite plans support reusable document templates for things such as:

  • offer letters;
  • agreements;
  • forms;
  • other documents requiring signatures, acknowledgements or approvals.

Templates can be built from DOCX or PDF files and include fields for completion/signature.

Notice what I’m not calling them:

legally compliant offer letters.

Gemini did when I attempted to use it to assist a student at work the other day.

I am not.

QuickBooks gives you a document/signature workflow. Whether the actual language in your offer letter complies with the laws applicable to that employer and employee is a human/legal question, not a magical property bestowed upon a PDF because QuickBooks touched it.

Gertrude appreciates this distinction.

Gertrude also refuses to sign anything without counsel present.

Learning: Yes, EasyLlama Is Actually Real

As of September 2026, QuickBooks has a Learning feature powered by EasyLlama. Employers can browse compliance and skills-training courses, assign them to employees with due dates, and track whether training is completed, overdue or not started. Employees access assigned training through Workforce and can download certificates for completed courses.

Current Intuit documentation says Learning is US-only and available with supported Workforce/payroll products, including Workforce Core, Premium and Elite, Intuit Enterprise Suite and Intuit Accountant Suite.

Time Tracking: Workforce Login Does Not Automatically Equal Clock-In Button

For supported subscriptions, team members can track time through Workforce/QuickBooks Time. Admins control settings and permissions including:

  • mobile time entry;
  • timesheet submission;
  • GPS;
  • mileage;
  • geofencing;
  • notifications;
  • Who’s Working;
  • mobile-device time zone.

If mobile time entry is disabled, that team member cannot clock in and out from their mobile device.

Employees/contractors who need to track time can be invited to Workforce from the time-team tools.

So the useful mental model is:

Workforce access tells us the person can access Workforce. Time-tracking permissions determine what they can do with Time.

Those aren’t the same thing.

Time Off Is Its Own Permission Story Too

Depending on the subscription and setup, employees can use Workforce for time-off activity as well.

Admins/managers can manage time-off requests, while employee ability to add/request time off depends on the applicable permissions and policies.

Again:

Feature exists does not mean every employee automatically has feature.

That distinction is going to save us approximately nine thousand stupid troubleshooting conversations.

🚨 Three Workforce Onboarding Traps

Trap 1: Treating the Invitation Like the Entire Onboarding Process

An employee needs an invitation to establish Workforce access.

They then need to accept it and set up/link their Intuit account.

But onboarding may involve additional steps depending on the employer’s payroll service and configuration.

So if somebody isn’t appearing where you expect them to appear, don’t invent a universal status called “Hire Pending” and assume clicking one magical Get Set Up button completes every Workforce workflow.

Check:

Was the worker actually added?

Were they invited?

Did they accept the invitation?

Are the relevant payroll/time products configured?

Were the necessary permissions enabled?

Troubleshooting works considerably better when we diagnose the actual layer that isn’t working.

Trap 2: January 3 Is Real

There really is a January 3 deadline associated with paperless W-2 delivery.

For supported payroll services, employees can opt out of mailed W-2 copies through Workforce through January 3. Intuit’s current 2026 year-end guidance also says employers using automated taxes/forms can avoid Intuit’s W-2 mailing fees by having employees choose paperless delivery or by changing the employer’s W-2 printing preference by that deadline.

Employees who choose paperless delivery review and accept a W-2 delivery consent notice.

What we’re not going to say is:

“Failing to hit Go Paperless automatically triggers a bulk physical printing run.”

The exact delivery workflow depends on the employer’s payroll setup.

Trap 3: Employee Tools and Admin Tools Are Different Jobs

Employees use Workforce for employee-facing activities.

Admins/managers configure payroll, permissions, time settings, employee records and other employer-side functions according to their access and subscriptions.

So if an employee can’t find an administrative control in their Workforce app:

Before declaring QuickBooks broken, ask:

Should this user even have that control?

Sometimes the missing button is the fucking feature.

🕵️ The Resistance Accountants Workforce Scavenger Hunt

The Scenario

Resistance Coffee & Books hires a new employee.

The worker has received a Workforce invitation, but tomorrow is their first shift and they’re supposed to clock in using their phone.

The manager says:

“They’re in Workforce. Why can’t they track time?”

Excellent question.

Task 1 — Establish Access

What must happen before the employee can actually use their Workforce account?

Task 2 — Find the Permission

The employee can sign in successfully but cannot clock in from the Workforce mobile app.

What admin-controlled permission should you investigate?

Task 3 — ¡Hola, Workforce!

The employee opens Workforce and discovers that the entire app is displaying in Spanish.

The company itself hasn’t suddenly relocated to Madrid.

What device setting should you check?

🛑 SCAVENGER HUNT ANSWERS

Task 1 — Accept and Set Up the Workforce Account

The employee needs to accept the employer’s Workforce invitation and sign in with an existing Intuit account or create/link one as needed.

What information they’re then able to enter themselves depends on the employer’s payroll service.

Task 2 — Check Mobile Time Entry

If the employee can use Workforce but can’t clock in from their phone, investigate Mobile time entry.

Intuit’s current documentation is refreshingly explicit:

When mobile time entry is turned off, that team member can’t clock in or out using their mobile device.

No database-token sorcery required.

Task 3 — Check the Phone’s Language

This is the tiny kernel of truth hiding inside Gemini’s bizarre original answer.

QuickBooks Workforce is available in Spanish, and Intuit says that when the mobile device language is set to Spanish, the Workforce app displays in Spanish too.

Device language → Workforce language?

Verified.

Device language → inexplicable payroll-currency corruption?

Go home, AI, you’re drunk.

Bonus — Access, Subscription and Permissions Are Separate Questions

A Workforce login doesn’t automatically grant every Workforce capability.

Features can depend on:

the employer’s subscription,

connected products,

the worker’s role,

company configuration,

and specific permissions.

That’s the troubleshooting framework I want you to remember.

Not:

“Workforce has this feature.”

Instead:

“Does this employer have this feature, and does this particular human have permission to use it?”

That’s much more useful.

So What Exactly Is Workforce Now?

Here’s the easiest way I’d hold Module 1 in your head:

Workforce is the employee-facing connection point.

Depending on the employer’s products and configuration, it can connect workers with:

pay information, tax documents, personal information, onboarding/document workflows, time tracking, time off, training and other workforce-management functions.

But the presence of the platform doesn’t erase:

subscription boundaries,

permissions,

employer responsibilities,

or human judgment.

In other words:

We have acquired another machine.

You know the rule.

Use the machine. Train the human.

And please don’t tell Gertrude she’s technically part of Human Capital Management now.

She’ll demand equity.
