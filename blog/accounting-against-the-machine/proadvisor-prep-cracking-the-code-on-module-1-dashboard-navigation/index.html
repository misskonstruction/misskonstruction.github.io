ProAdvisor Prep: Cracking the Code on Module 1 — Dashboard Navigation

August 27, 2026

So you’ve created your QBOA account.

Excellent.

Now we have to actually open the damn thing.

Welcome to Module 1: dashboard navigation—the part where QuickBooks introduces you to approximately 47 places you can click and then waits patiently to see what you’ll do.

This first module isn’t particularly glamorous, but don’t blow through it because it looks easy. Knowing where things live is what eventually lets you stop hunting through menus like you’ve lost your keys.

We’re learning the geography first.

Then we can start breaking shit.

Preferably in the sample company.

First Things First: Don’t Try to Memorize the Whole Damn Screen

When you first open QuickBooks Online Accountant, there’s a lot going on.

Navigation menus. Clients. Settings. Transactions. Search. Accountant tools. Training. Your own firm. Somebody else’s company.

You don’t need to memorize all of it.

And depending on when you’re reading this, your QBOA/Intuit Accountant Suite interface may not look exactly like mine because Intuit updates the platform and moves things around.

That’s okay.

The important thing in Module 1 is understanding what kind of workspace you’re in, what the major navigation areas do, and where you would logically start looking for something.

You’re building a mental map.

The Most Important Distinction: Whose Books Am I In?

If you remember absolutely nothing else from this post, remember this:

Know whose company you’re working in before you start doing shit.

QBOA exists so accounting professionals can manage their own practice while also accessing client companies.

That means you’re going to move between different contexts.

Your firm is one thing.

Your client’s company is another.

This sounds painfully obvious until you’ve spent twenty minutes doing something and realize you were doing it in the wrong place.

Before entering, changing, deleting, reconciling, adjusting, or otherwise touching anything important, get into the habit of checking which company you’re actually in.

Future You will appreciate this.

So will your clients.

Learn What the Major Navigation Areas Are For

Don’t approach navigation by trying to memorize:

Button A is here. Button B is there.

Interfaces change.

Instead, learn the logic behind the tools.

Creating Transactions

When you’re looking for tools to create transactions, you’re looking for the area QuickBooks provides for actions such as invoices, expenses, bills, checks, deposits, journal entries, and other financial activity.

Depending on the version and interface you’re using, the exact navigation may vary.

The important question to start asking yourself is:

Am I trying to CREATE something?

That’s your clue.

Settings and Configuration

This is different.

If you’re trying to manage the company’s setup, users, accounts, preferences, imports, or other structural pieces of the company, you’re no longer asking QuickBooks to record an everyday transaction.

You’re configuring the environment.

Start separating those concepts in your head now:

Transaction = something happened.

Configuration = how QuickBooks is set up to handle the things that happen.

That distinction becomes much more useful than memorizing where an icon happens to live today.

And Then There Are Accountant Tools

This is where QBOA starts becoming more interesting.

Accountants and bookkeepers need tools that regular users don’t necessarily need every day. As you work through your training, you’ll encounter accountant-specific functionality designed to help you review, correct, reconcile, analyze, and manage client books more efficiently.

Don’t worry about mastering all of it in Module 1.

Right now I want you to know it exists.

We’re going to come back and play with the good shit later.

DO NOT Practice Your Curiosity in Brenda’s Actual Company

I said this in the introductory post and I’m going to keep saying it because it’s important:

CLICK ON SHIT.

But there is a very important second half to that statement:

NOT IN A LIVE CLIENT’S BOOKS.

QuickBooks provides sample/demo environments specifically so you can explore without giving an actual business owner an aneurysm.

Use them.

Click buttons.

Open menus.

Create transactions.

Run reports.

Change something and see what happens.

Ask yourself, “I wonder what this does?”

Then find out.

That’s how you learn software.

If you screw up the sample company, congratulations.

You have successfully harmed absolutely no one.

Three Beginner Mistakes Worth Avoiding

1. Memorizing Click Paths Instead of Understanding the Workflow

Knowing that something currently takes four clicks through a particular menu is useful.

Understanding why you’re going there is better.

If the interface changes later—and eventually it will—you can still find your way around because you understand what you’re trying to accomplish.

2. Experimenting in Live Books

No.

Sample company.

Go wreak havoc there.

3. Forgetting Which Company You’re In

Before you do anything consequential, look at your screen and verify the company.

Make it a habit now.

Eventually you’ll do it without thinking.

That’s the goal of Module 1: not memorizing a screenshot, but developing enough familiarity that navigating QBO stops consuming your mental bandwidth.

Your First QBO Scavenger Hunt

Reading about navigation is boring.

So we’re not stopping there.

Open the practice/sample environment available to you and see if you can locate each of these without immediately running back to the training material:

  • Find where you would create an invoice.
  • Find where you would record an expense.
  • Locate the Chart of Accounts.
  • Find where reports live.
  • Find the area used for bank transactions or connected accounts.
  • Locate the company settings.
  • Find the tools available specifically to an accountant.
  • Figure out how to tell which company you’re currently viewing.
  • Return to your accountant/firm environment.
  • Find your ProAdvisor training progress again.

Bonus round: Pick one thing you found and explain to yourself why it belongs where it does.

Yes, seriously.

If you can explain it, you’re considerably closer to understanding it.

One More Thing: Get Lost

I’m serious.

Once you’ve completed the official Module 1 material, give yourself 15 minutes in the sample environment with no instructions.

Go exploring.

Find three things that weren’t covered in the lesson.

Open a report you’ve never seen.

Look at a transaction and follow where it goes.

Find something you don’t understand and write down the question.

Getting a little lost in a safe environment is one of the fastest ways to stop being afraid of software.

And if you’re training with other people, make it competitive:

Who can find the requested feature first—and explain what it actually does?

Congratulations.

We have accidentally invented a QBO scavenger hunt.

There will be more.

What I’m Actually Studying for Here

The goal isn’t:

Pass Module 1.

Obviously, yes, I want to pass the certification.

But I’m trying to come out the other side actually knowing QBO better.

There’s a difference.

I don’t want to memorize an answer long enough to select it on an assessment and immediately dump it from my brain.

I want to understand the software well enough that when somebody says:

“Something is wrong and I don’t know what I did,”

I know where to start looking.

That’s the skill.

The certification documents it.

Module 1 Mission

Finish the official training.

Then close it.

Open the practice environment.

Spend at least 15 minutes navigating without instructions.

Get lost.

Find your way back.

Click on something unfamiliar.

Write down at least one question.

If you can move around the environment with less hesitation than you could when you started, Module 1 did its job.

And if you still feel lost?

Good.

Now you know what you need to practice.

I’m working through this certification too, so I’ll keep documenting what I learn, what trips me up, what actually helps me remember it, and whatever training activities we come up with along the way.

We’re not racing for a badge.

We’re building humans who know what the hell they’re doing.

Welcome back, Resistance Accountant.

Now go click on shit.
