My Service Dog Training Chronicles

November 20, 2026

Update: I’ve Been Going to School, and Apparently I’m a Very Good Girl! 🐾💗

Hello, everyone!

It’s me, Gladys. Former shelter girl, professional bed thief, and now officially a student.

That’s right. I’m going to service dog school!

Mother says I’m learning to become a medical alert service dog, which means I’ll eventually be trained to recognize certain changes in her breathing and heart rate so I can help her when something isn’t right.

That’s a pretty important job for a girl who spends a considerable amount of time upside down with her tongue hanging out.

But apparently, I’m doing exceptionally well!

First, We Had to Charge My Marker

Now, before anyone gets excited, nobody was electrocuted.

A marker is a special word Mother uses to tell me I’ve done something right. She chose YES!

My first assignment was to learn that whenever I hear that word, something wonderful happens.

Usually bacon-flavored wonderful.

We started on November 8, in Mother’s bedroom, where it was quiet and we could concentrate.

She said YES! and gave me a treat. Then she said it again and gave me another.

I thought this was a fantastic educational program.

Eventually, she started saying YES! when I wasn’t paying attention, and I learned to turn my head toward her.

Later that day, we even practiced at Home Depot!

Imagine that. A former shelter dog attending class in a hardware store.

I didn’t even need to buy lumber.

Then Mother Got Sneaky

By November 9, Mother decided to test me while she was making dinner.

She’d been practicing my marker while doing other things, and I was getting pretty good at responding.

Then I settled down for a nap.

For approximately three glorious minutes, nobody bothered me.

Until suddenly…

YES!

My head snapped around so fast you’d have thought someone had announced a bacon emergency.

Mother was delighted.

I was hoping for a snack.

Everybody won.

Distractions? What Distractions?

On November 13, Mother made things a little more challenging.

My dog brother was present, and there were some mild distractions.

Now, you might think having another dog nearby would make it difficult to concentrate.

Not me.

Every time Mother used my marker, I gave her my attention.

She wrote it down in my practice log, too.

Apparently, I’m very impressive.

I would like this information forwarded to the treat department.

My Name Is Gladys, and I Know It!

Next came learning to respond to my name and make eye contact.

You see, I’ve had several names in my life.

I had a name before I went to the shelter, another temporary name while I was there, and then Mother adopted me and decided I was a Gladys.

Personally, I think Gladys suits me beautifully.

On November 14, we practiced with another human and another dog nearby.

Mother waited until I was looking away, then called my name.

And guess what?

I looked right at her!

Sometimes I looked at the hand holding the treats instead.

Look, I’m a dog. Let’s be reasonable.

Mother caught on and started hiding the treats beside her or behind her back.

Sneaky woman.

But even when I appeared to lose interest, she’d call my name and I’d snap right back to making eye contact.

She was very proud of me.

I was very proud of the treats.

November 20: Another Gold Star for Gladys!

Today we practiced again, and Mother wrote something especially nice in my training log.

She says I’ve fully mastered responding to my name and making eye contact.

Not bad for a girl who’s been called three different things in her lifetime!

We’ve also discovered that Blue Buffalo treats work particularly well for training without contributing to additional weight gain.

Mother seems concerned about my figure.

I think I’m magnificent.

But if smaller treats mean I can have more of them, I’m willing to cooperate.

She finished today’s entry by writing:

“She is exceptional.”

Well.

I don’t want to brag, but…

Actually, yes, I do.

I AM EXCEPTIONAL! 💗

My First Completed Lesson Is Almost Here!

All this practicing has been preparing me to complete my first official lesson.

We’ve been working on understanding how I learn, responding to my marker, recognizing my name, and giving Mother my full attention even when other things are happening around us.

These might sound like little things, but they’re the foundation for the much bigger job I’ll eventually be doing.

Mother says I need to learn to pay attention to her because one day she may need me to recognize when something is wrong.

And that’s something I take seriously.

Well, as seriously as a bulldog can take anything while simultaneously wondering whether there’s cheese in the refrigerator.

Mother Says She’s Proud of Me

I know Mother loves me whether I learn a hundred commands or spend the rest of my life snoring on her pillow.

But I can tell how happy she gets when I learn something new.

She smiles, praises me, and tells me what a wonderful girl I am.

Sometimes she gets so excited you’d think I’d just graduated from Harvard.

And maybe, in our own little way, that’s exactly what we’re working toward.

I have a long way to go before I’m fully trained as a medical alert service dog.

There will be more lessons, new challenges, and probably a few moments when I decide a squirrel is more interesting than higher education.

But we’re doing this together.

And Mother says she couldn’t be prouder of how far I’ve come already.

From a shelter girl learning to trust people to a service dog student learning how to look after her favorite human.

That’s a pretty beautiful beginning, don’t you think?

Now, if you’ll excuse me, I believe I’ve earned a nap.

And possibly a diploma made of bacon.

Love,

Gladys 🐾💗

Service dog student. Eye contact expert. Professional snack enthusiast.
