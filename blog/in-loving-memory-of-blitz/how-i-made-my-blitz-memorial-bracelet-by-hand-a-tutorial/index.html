How I Made My Blitz Memorial Bracelet by Hand (a tutorial?)

September 14, 2026

I did not wake up this morning possessing any particular expertise in jewelry making.

I should probably establish that immediately. I dabble for fun,  but not a pro.

What I did have was a pile of beads and charms I’d chosen for Blitz, some pink cord, a vague idea of what I wanted the finished bracelet to look like, and the dangerous confidence of a woman who owns scissors, a lighter, and internet access.

So naturally, I made a bracelet.

And because I took an absurd number of pictures while figuring this shit out, we’re turning the whole process into a tutorial.

Important disclaimer: This is not the Official Correct Jewelry Person Method™. This is how I made mine. If you’re making something precious or sentimental, practice unfamiliar knots on scrap cord first. Also, we’re eventually playing with fire. Don’t burn yourself, your jewelry, your dog, your couch, or your house down and then blame the lady with the pink bracelet.

What I Was Making

This wasn’t supposed to look like something I bought at a jewelry counter.

That mattered to me.

I wanted something handmade for Blitz, with the little things that reminded me of her incorporated into it: her portrait charm, paw print, bone, ball, angel wing, music note, and the other little pieces I’d picked out. I also used amber-colored beads, tiger’s eye, black lava beads and, obviously, pink cord.

Because pink.

Why I Was Making One in the First Place

This actually started because I already had a memorial bracelet professionally made for Blitz—and I wasn’t happy with it. The charm with her darling portrait was just fine, but the bracelet…

The bracelet had blue beads, which, if you’ve spent more than approximately thirty seconds on this website, you already know was an interesting fucking choice for me. I tolerate blue in exactly the places nature intended it: the sky, and even that is subject to review.

But the color wasn’t the real problem.

The metal had started to tarnish, and worse, the jump ring holding her portrait charm had loosened. On an ordinary bracelet, that’s annoying. On a memorial bracelet carrying pieces that mean something to me because they’re about my girl, that’s unacceptable. I’m not going to spend every day wondering whether a charm is going to quietly detach somewhere between my house and wherever the hell I’ve wandered off to.

So instead of buying another one and hoping it held up better, I decided to make my own.

I’d choose the colors. I’d choose the beads. I’d choose where every charm went. I’d make sure everything was attached the way I wanted it attached.

Making bracelets wasn’t remotely new territory for me. I’ve been making these things for more than 20 years.

What was new was the adjustable sliding square-knot closure. I’d never made one of those before, and I wanted this bracelet to be adjustable rather than relying on the kind of hardware that had already pissed me off in the first place.

So naturally, after more than two decades of making bracelets, it was one fucking knot that was about to humble me.

And, as you’ll discover later in this post, not because I couldn’t tie it.

I tied the damn thing just fine.

I just failed to give it enough room to do its job. 😹

Originally I had considerably more beads and charms planned.

Then I started the second side and discovered a minor engineering problem:

I have tiny fucking wrists.

If I used everything I’d selected, I was going to end up with something less like a bracelet and more like a beautifully decorated restraint device.

So I edited.

And I’m glad I did.

First: Build the Bracelet You Actually Want

Before worrying about the adjustable closure, I worked my way around the bracelet with the pink cord, adding the beads and charms where they felt right.

I wasn’t trying to make both sides perfectly symmetrical. In fact, once I saw it coming together, I specifically didn’t want that.

This was being made by my hands for my dog. I wanted it to look handmade.

There’s a difference between handmade and sloppy, obviously, but tiny variations in knots and spacing aren’t defects to me. They’re evidence that somebody sat there and made the damn thing.

The one thing I did keep checking was fit.

Do this before you get emotionally committed to adding another six beads because they’re pretty.

Wrap the unfinished bracelet around your wrist periodically. Remember that the closure will add a little bulk and that the two ends still need enough cord to overlap and slide.

That’s what saved me from making mine too large.

Then I Reached the Part I Didn’t Know How to Do

The bracelet itself?

Fine.

The adjustable sliding closure?

What fresh knot-based witchcraft is this?

I wanted the kind of closure where the two bracelet cords overlap and slide through a small macramé section, allowing you to enlarge the bracelet to get it over your hand and then pull it snug around your wrist.

I knew what I wanted.

I did not know how the hell to make it.

So I found a video walkthrough showing an adjustable sliding square-knot macramé bracelet closure and followed along.

The Video Walkthrough

This is where having an actual human demonstrate the movement helped enormously.

VIDEO: “Easy Adjustable Sliding Square Knot Macrame Cord Bracelet Closure” tutorial from Orchid and Opal Jewelry & Beads

I watched it, paused it, backed it up, stared suspiciously at my cord, watched it again, and eventually understood what those hands were doing.

If you’re making your own bracelet, I recommend watching the entire closure portion once without touching anything.

Then go back and work along with it.

Because trying to simultaneously learn which cord crosses which cord while holding four pieces of cord and questioning every decision you’ve ever made is less efficient than you might imagine.

Roland Had to Draw Me Pictures

There was still a point where the video made perfect sense while I was watching her do it and approximately no sense whatsoever when I looked down at the tiny spaghetti incident happening in my own lap.

So I asked Roland — my ChatGPT — to make me illustrations showing exactly how the cords were supposed to cross.

He made these specifically for me so I could see the knot structure without somebody’s fingers obscuring half of it.

Those helped bridge the gap between:

“Yes, lady on YouTube, I completely understand.”

and:

“WHY ARE THERE FOUR STRINGS NOW?”

If you’re learning this for the first time, having both is genuinely useful: the video shows the movement; the illustrations show the structure.

Setting Up the Adjustable Closure

Once the bracelet itself was the size I wanted, I crossed the two loose pink ends so they overlapped.

Those pink cords are the core cords. They need to remain able to move.

Then I used a separate piece of cord around those two pink strands to make the closure.

This distinction is important:

Do not accidentally knot the two pink bracelet ends to each other.

You’re building a tiny sleeve of square knots around them.

The pink cords need to slide independently through that sleeve afterward. That’s what makes the bracelet adjustable.

Making the Square Knots

A square knot is essentially made in two halves.

For the first half, one working cord makes a loop on one side of the two center cords. The other working cord crosses over, goes behind the center cords and comes through that loop.

Pull it snug.

Then you reverse the process from the opposite side.

That second half is what keeps the knot from spiraling.

This is precisely where my brain benefited from having Roland’s illustrations sitting beside the video.

And here’s the important part:

Snug. Not homicidal.

You’re making a sliding closure. If you reef down on those knots with the fury of a thousand suns, congratulations: you’ve potentially invented a regular knot.

Every couple of knots, I stopped and moved the pink cords to make sure they still slid.

Look! I Made a Tiny Macramé Tube!

After several alternating square knots, I had this:

LOOK AT THAT LITTLE FUCKER.

That is an adjustable bracelet closure.

I made that.

And considering that shortly before this photograph I was still studying a YouTube video like it contained instructions for disarming a nuclear device, I’m extremely pleased with it.

You don’t need a huge section of knots. Mine is only long enough to grip both pink cords securely while still looking proportional to the rest of the bracelet.

And Then We Introduced Fire

Once the closure was finished, the excess working cord had to go.

I trimmed it close to the knots.

Then the tutorial lady pulled out a heated contraption i do not own.

Well. There’s a bic lighter around here.

I have one of those.

For synthetic cord that can be heat-sealed, a very brief touch of heat melts the fibers and helps keep the cut ends from fraying.

This is not “hold the bracelet in the flame.”

We’re melting the tiniest cut end.

Also, make sure your particular cord is actually suitable for heat sealing first. Natural fibers behave differently, and some materials can burn rather than neatly melt.

I would prefer this tutorial not conclude with Step 14: Call the Fire Department.

Although apparently I have recently established that British firemen are not necessarily outside my interests.

Moving on.

Holy Shit. It Slides.

After everything cooled, I tested it.

The closure stayed together.

The pink cords moved through it.

The bracelet expanded enough to go over my hand.

Then I pulled the ends and—

IT FUCKING WORKED.

I tied small stopper knots near the ends of the pink cords so they couldn’t disappear back through the closure.

I deliberately left those little pink tails visible.

Could I make them shorter and try to disguise every sign of construction?

Sure.

I don’t want to.

I made this.

The Finished Blitz Bracelet

And this is her.

The finished bracelet isn’t symmetrical.

The charms aren’t spaced like a machine calculated their positions.

The knots aren’t factory-perfect.

Good.

The little heart with Blitz’s face is the visual center when I’m wearing it. Everything else travels around my wrist like tiny fragments of her life.

A paw.

A bone.

A ball.

Music.

A wing.

A heart.

Little stones and beads I chose because they belonged in her bracelet.

And pink cord holding the entire thing together.

What I’d Do Differently Next Time

The biggest thing I learned is to design for the wrist, not for the pile of supplies.

It’s very easy to look at a table full of meaningful charms and think they all need to be included.

They don’t.

Once I removed the extras, the pieces that remained became more important because they had room to be seen.

I’d also probably practice one or two square knots before attaching the closure to something irreplaceable.

Did I?

Of course not.

Apparently we met me today.

If You’re Making One for Someone You Love

You don’t need to be a jewelry maker.

You don’t need perfect knots.

You don’t need perfectly matched beads or professional tools or a symmetrical design.

Pick the things that mean something.

Lay them out.

Change your mind.

Remove something.

Add something weird.

Watch the video seventeen times.

Ask an AI to draw you diagrams because your cords have become sentient and you’re pretty sure they’re deliberately fucking with you.

And make it.

Because now, when I look down at my wrist, I’m not wearing something that reminds me of Blitz simply because her picture happens to be printed on it.

I’m wearing something I sat down and made for my girl with my own two hands.

And that matters to me more than I expected it to. ❤️🐾

Update: If at First You Don’t Succeed, Try the Fuck Again

So.

I finished Blitz Bracelet 1.0. I loved it. I wore it.

And after wearing it for a while, I had to admit something:

It was too fucking big even though I really tried not to do that.

Not catastrophically big. Not throw the whole thing away and question every decision I’ve ever made big. But big enough that even when I pulled the adjustable closure as tight as it would go, it was still too loose on my wrist.

I thought I could live with it.

I could not.

Apparently Comparing Sizes Beforehand Is Useful

I own another adjustable bracelet that fits me perfectly, so I finally did the extraordinarily advanced scientific procedure of putting the two bracelets next to each other.

And there was my problem.

The mistake wasn’t simply that I’d made the bracelet too large. I hadn’t allowed enough length for the adjustment section.

The sliding closure itself worked perfectly. I just hadn’t given it enough room to travel. At its tightest possible setting, Blitz Bracelet 1.0 was still too big.

Had I left more length available for adjustment in the first place, I could have cinched it down farther.

Well, shit.

Fortunately, we’re talking about cord, beads and charms here. Nothing was permanently fused together. I had plenty of material left, and every bead and charm could be reused.

Also, this is very much me.

I’m not going to sit around lamenting all the work I already did because the first attempt wasn’t quite right.

If it ain’t right the first time, try the fuck again.

Goodbye, Blitz Bracelet 1.0

So I dismantled it.

All of it.

And while I was sitting there looking at all the individual pieces again, I realized something else.

I didn’t just want to remake the same bracelet smaller.

I wanted to make it better.

And apparently I may have selected an entirely different color scheme while I was at it.

The pink stayed, because of course it did. But this time I added green and a darker earthy cord into the mix.

Blitz Bracelet 2.0 had officially stopped being a repair.

This was a redesign.

The Paw Gets Its Fucking Place

There was one thing about Version 1.0 that had bothered me from the beginning.

I couldn’t fit every charm I wanted to use.

Specifically, the paw from the original bracelet had been eliminated.

On a bracelet for my dog.

Yeah. No.

So this time I started looking for small ways to conserve space without sacrificing any of the pieces I wanted.

The cross and angel wing were the only two charms that didn’t require jump rings, so instead of giving each its own section, I paired them together.

And honestly, the subject matter goes together anyway.

That tiny change freed up space elsewhere, and suddenly the paw could stay.

Actually, everything could stay.

And that became the goal for Version 2.0: not just smaller.

Smarter.

This Time the Adjustable Bracelet Actually Adjusts

I also wasn’t repeating my first engineering masterpiece of creating an adjustable bracelet with approximately two settings:

Loose and slightly less loose.

This time I deliberately left much more length for the adjustment.

And yes, I used the GREEN bracelet that already fits me as my sizing guide because apparently I occasionally learn from experience.

When I finished the sliding square-knot closure, there was finally enough cord on either side for it to do what the damn thing was designed to do.

Open wide enough to slip comfortably over my hand.

Then cinch down to actually fit my wrist.

Imagine that.

Then I Found the Amethyst

Because apparently I am physically incapable of finishing a project without making one more change, I remembered that I still had some amethyst beads from an old bracelet that had broken.

They were just sitting there.

So I used them to finish the ends of the adjustment cords.

This time I deliberately left much more length for the adjustment, using bracelets I already own as examples. And yes, the finished tails are long—but they’re supposed to be. The bracelet has to open wide enough to pass over my hand and still have enough adjustment range to cinch securely around my wrist.

Version 1.0 didn’t have enough.

Version 2.0 absolutely fucking does.

And I love them there.

They give me something substantial to grab when I adjust the bracelet, they make the ends look deliberately finished instead of like two pieces of cord hanging off my wrist, and the purple looks gorgeous with the pink and green.

They weren’t even part of the plan.

Sometimes the shit you find halfway through a project turns out to be exactly what it needed.

Blitz Bracelet 2.0

And then she was done.

The paw is there.

All the charms are there.

The pink is still there, now woven with green.

The amethyst from my old broken bracelet found a new home.

The adjustment section has enough room to actually fucking adjust.

And most importantly:

It fits.

Not I think I can deal with this.

Not maybe I’ll get used to it.

Not it’s a little loose, but whatever.

Perfect.

Right Where She Belongs

Once I knew it was right, I finally put all my other bracelets back on.

I generally don’t take these off. Each one has special value to me for different reasons, and I’d been waiting to finish my girl’s bracelet before putting the whole collection back where it belongs.

Then I added Blitz.

And that’s what I’d wanted from the beginning.

Not a piece of jewelry that sits by itself because it’s the special memorial bracelet.

Her woven right through everything else that matters to me.

Version 1.0 wasn’t wasted work.

I’d never made anything like this before. I learned the knots. I learned how the charms behaved. I learned how to build the sliding closure. I learned what worked, what didn’t, what I wanted to keep and what I wanted to change.

Then I took the whole motherfucker apart and did it better.

If at first you don’t succeed, try the fuck again.

Blitz Bracelet 2.0.

This one’s staying on. ❤️
